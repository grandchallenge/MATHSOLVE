Preserve one-bit decision semantics and arbitrary nonlinear reuse.

## 1. Target-specific transport

Let B be the Boolean function ring over the binary field. For target f define J=fB (functions supported on the one-fiber), with induced filtration J_r=J intersect F_r and graded ideal M=gr J embedded in A=gr B. For a legal gate g=uv put q=[g] in grade two and use the established law F'_r=F_r+gF_{r-1}; J'_r=J intersect F'_r. Negative grades vanish. The quotient filtration on B/J is the restriction filtration on the zero-fiber.

Define the graded promoted target pieces by

$$
K_r=qA_{r-1}\cap M_{r+1}.
$$

For r>=0 the old leading-symbol map identifies F'_r/F_r with qA_{r-1} in A_{r+1}. Its restriction identifies J'_r/J_r with K_r: if a representative h in F'_r has old symbol in M_{r+1}, choose j in J intersect F_{r+1} with that symbol; h+j belongs to F_r, hence j belongs to F'_r. The converse and kernel are immediate. The identity on J induces a graded map M to M' (the new graded ideal). Its degree-r kernel is J'_{r-1}/J_{r-1} and cokernel J'_r/J_r. Consequently

$$
0\to K_{r-1}\to M_r\to M'_r\to K_r\to0.
$$

The intersection contains qM, but equality need not hold. The defect

$$
D_f(q)=((q)\cap M)/(qM)
$$

is a graded A-module annihilated by q, canonically Tor_1^A(A/(q),A/M) by tensoring 0 -> (q) -> A -> A/(q) -> 0 with A/M: the kernel of (q)/(qM) -> A/M is the stated quotient. This measures failure of intersection to be generated by products of target relations; it is not a computational cost theorem.

## 2. Minimal generators and spectator invariance

For a graded A-module N use the minimal generator space N/A_+N, where A_+ is the positive-degree ideal. A is connected and generated in degree one, so the grade-r generator multiplicity of M is

$$
\beta_r=\dim(M_r/A_1M_{r-1}).
$$

For independent unused variables, the affine spectator filtration has associated graded exterior algebra E. The lifted target ideal has tensor-product filtration (expand in square-free spectator monomials, use coefficient uniqueness), hence the graded target module is M tensor E over the binary field. If the gate also ignores spectators, qA, M, their intersection and product all tensor with E by exactness of tensoring vector spaces. The defect tensors with E as well. Quotienting either module by the positive-degree ideal of A tensor E cancels all positive spectator grades, giving the original minimal generator space. Thus these profiles are spectator invariant, unlike raw dimension.

## 3. Exponential one-gate promotion without spectators

Let n=m+2, with coordinates a,b,z_1,...,z_m, m>=2, and k=floor(m/2). Define the single Boolean target f=ab AND Thr_{m,k}(z), where the threshold is one if at least k z bits are one. Every coordinate is essential. There is no nonzero translation period, including after invertible affine changes of coordinates: for any monotone Boolean function, invariance under flipping a nonempty set S implies equality of values at all-zero and all-one S for each fixed complement, so monotonicity makes every S coordinate irrelevant. This target is monotone and essential in every coordinate. It is inexpensive: compute T_{i,j}=T_{i-1,j} OR (z_i AND T_{i-1,j-1}), with T_{i,0}=1 and T_{0,j}=0 for j>0; each update costs at most two multiplications (OR is x+y+xy), then two further multiplications form ab and the target. Bound: 2mk+2.

At the affine state the graded ring is exterior. Square-free monomial truth functions form a basis of B. For a monotone target the monomials with support a true input form a basis of J: each is supported on the up-set, they are independent, and their count equals the number of true inputs, the dimension of J. Intersecting with the ordinary degree filtration preserves the monomial basis. Here M has basis ab z_T for |T|>=k, with binomial(m,k) minimal generators ab z_T, |T|=k, in degree k+2.

Adjoin the single gate g=ab. Then q=[ab], M is contained in (q), and qM=0. Therefore D_f(q)=M with binomial(m,k) minimal generators, and dimension sum_{j=k}^m binomial(m,j). The count is at least 2^m/(m+1). This is exponential gate-local defect even after removing spectators and counting only minimal generators.

Moreover the same number of minimal generators in the new target module occur in degree k+1, represented by t z_T. After adjoining ab each monomial has least filtration degree equal to ordinary degree minus one if it contains both a,b, otherwise ordinary degree. The upper bound uses the new generator ab; the lower bound follows by expanding products of available monomials, where ab covers at most one extra coordinate. Independence rules out cancellations producing missing monomials. The target filtration is the span of true-support monomials of that weighted degree. The |T|=k generators are in the lowest nonzero grade and all others are generated by multiplication with remaining z coordinates, increasing grade by one per factor.

Consider the nonnegative graded workload

$$
L(V,f)=\sum_r\max(r-1,0)\beta_r.
$$

It is spectator invariant but a single gate decreases it by binomial(m,k), exponential on a polynomial-size target with no affine spectator factor. The raw number of minimal generators does not change; no theorem excluding every nonlinear or ungraded generator functional is claimed.

## 4. Target completion is a separate obstruction

Even L=0 when f is available fails. On four bits a,b,c,d let V contain affine inputs and computed gates ab and c(a+d). Take f=d, already an input. J_1 has basis d, since restricting all other listed basis elements to d=0 gives independent 1,a,b,c,ab,ac. The subspace of J_2 generated by degree-one target elements has basis d,ad,bd,cd,abd,acd: multiply d by available functions and use d^2=d. But bcd belongs to F_2 because b*c(a+d)+c*ab=bcd, and abcd belongs to F_2 because (ab)*c(a+d)+c*ab=abcd. These two independent additional monomials give all eight dimensions of J in F_2. Thus beta_1=1, beta_2=2, and all other betas vanish. In particular L(V,d)=2 despite zero remaining target gates. The target ideal encodes more than the decision bit. Forcing zero at completion does not repair the exponential drop in Section 3, where f is not in either degree-one state.
