## 1. Safe products and the constrained exterior image

Let B=F2^X, X=F2^n, V contain the affine input functions, and let H be any linear subspace containing V. A safe gate is a product uv of available functions u,v in V with uv in H. Put E=V/span(1) and let mu: exterior^2 E -> B/V send u wedge v to uv+V, well defined by idempotence and bilinearity. The span of safe gates modulo V is exactly
    mu(span{q decomposable: mu(q) in H/V}).
This is an exact constrained shared-label image, not the unrestricted linear image of the intersection with H/V.

Specialize to H=ker(lambda), for a nonzero functional lambda annihilating V. Let beta(u,v)=lambda(uv), an alternating form on E. Then the span of decomposable q with beta(q)=0 equals the entire kernel of the linear functional beta on exterior^2 E. If beta=0 this follows from the exterior basis. Otherwise choose a symplectic basis e_i,f_i for the nondegenerate part, and radical vectors. Such a basis exists by splitting a pair with beta(e,f)=1 and taking its orthogonal complement recursively. Every elementary exterior basis pair except e_i wedge f_i is beta-zero. A linear combination belongs to ker beta iff the sum of the coefficients of the exceptional pairs is zero. Their even sums are generated by pairwise differences (here sums). For i != j,
    e_i wedge f_i + e_j wedge f_j
      = (e_i+e_j) wedge (f_i+f_j) + e_i wedge f_j + e_j wedge f_i.
Each term on the right is beta-zero and decomposable. Thus the spanning assertion holds. In particular, a list of O(dim(V)^2) safe products suffices for the span, obtained from an alternating basis and the displayed two-pair constructions (one fixed exceptional pair suffices).

Since mu(exterior^2 E)=F_2(V)/V and beta=lambda composed with mu, the safe span is exactly
    V + span{uv: u,v in V, lambda(uv)=0} = H intersect F_2(V).
This is not merely a linear relaxation: the span is realizable by sequential legal gates, each using operands already in V. A basis can be selected from actual safe products, omitting dependent ones. No complexity in n is asserted: dim(V) may be exponential. The one-constraint decomposable spanning lemma does not extend automatically to multiple constraints.

## 2. Every containing hyperplane is reachable

Theorem: if V is strictly contained in H=ker(lambda) with nonzero lambda, there exists a safe gate uv in H minus V. Repeating nonredundant safe gates reaches H in exactly dim(H)-dim(V) charged gates, all staying inside H.

Proof. Suppose no safe gate leaves V. Section 1 gives H intersect F_2(V)=V. If lambda vanishes on F_2(V), then F_2(V)=V and V is a unital subalgebra separating X (contains all input coordinates), hence B: the product of coordinate literals is each singleton indicator. This contradicts V subset H proper.

Otherwise F_2(V)=V+span(g), where g=uv with lambda(g)=1, since H cuts F_2(V) in exactly one dimension. This space is also a unital subalgebra. Old products lie in it by definition. For w in V, write vw=w_0+alpha*g with w_0 in V, alpha in F2. Then
    gw=u(vw)=u w_0+alpha*u g=u w_0+alpha*g in F_2(V),
because u^2=u. Also g^2=g. Thus F_2(V) separates points and equals B. Its dimension is dim(V)+1, so V=H, a contradiction. A safe nonredundant gate exists. Each such addition increases dimension by exactly one and retains affine inputs. Iterate to H. Any history attaining H has at least this many gates by dimension, so the length is exact, though typically exponential in n.

This result does not assert that every intermediate subspace is reachable or that safe sequences can be found in polynomial time in n. Given explicit truth tables, section 1 gives an algebraic safe-gate selection procedure polynomial in the truth-table size using alternating-basis reduction and linear elimination.

## 3. General subspace saturation and a counterexample

For general H define T_H(U)=U+span{uv:u,v in U, uv in H}. Starting from V, iterate T_H. The sequence increases and stabilizes at a unique least fixed point containing V. Every batch is realized by a sequence of legal nonredundant gates staying in H, by selecting an independent subset of its safe products. Conversely, every H-contained history remains in the fixed point by induction on its operands. Hence this fixed point is exactly the maximal reachable feature space inside H, not just an upper estimate. Hyperplanes containing affine inputs have fixed point H by section 2.

An arbitrary containing subspace need not be reachable. On four inputs a,b,c,d, start at the affine space V and take H=V+span(ab+cd). Any nonzero gate class from V is a decomposable exterior 2-form. The quadratic class ab+cd has alternating matrix rank four, so is not decomposable. Consequently there are no safe nonredundant gates; H is strictly larger than its reachable fixed point V. This also shows the one-constraint spanning lemma fails for general linear constraints: the allowed exterior subspace span(a wedge b+c wedge d) has no nonzero decomposable element. No general efficient constrained-saturation algorithm is claimed.

## 4. Exact fixed-family completeness characterization

If f notin V, let Lambda={lambda in V^perp: lambda(f)=1}. It is an affine coset of size M=2^(2^n-dim(V)-1). A fixed subfamily S subset Lambda is completion-sound across ALL legal continuations from V (extinction of S implies f available) iff S=Lambda.

Sufficiency is ordinary dual separation. For necessity choose any omitted lambda in Lambda. By section 2 reach H=ker(lambda) by a legal safe history from V. Since H^perp=span(lambda), the surviving target-disagreeing witness coset is precisely {lambda}. Every member of S is dead, yet f is not in H. This holds even for a one-gate target such as ab from affine inputs. There are exactly M distinct target-avoiding hyperplanes containing V, each reachable by exactly 2^n-1-dim(V) nonredundant gates. No efficient computation of the target is being excluded: the adversarial history is long. This sharply strengthens the necessary cardinality estimate in JOINT_WITNESS_ELIMINATION.md; no dependence on target complexity is needed for this ALL-HISTORIES completeness requirement.

For the affine starting space on n>=2 bits, M=2^(2^n-n-2), doubly exponential in n even for AND of two coordinates. This does not say AND is difficult. It says extinction tests requiring soundness across arbitrary reachable states cannot discard any individual witness. In particular any predicate of the surviving fixed subfamily alone that accepts its empty set as completion is unsound unless the subfamily is full. A uniform sample of s distinct witnesses has probability exactly s/M of detecting the surviving singleton at a fixed target-avoiding hyperplane; with replacement that probability is 1-(1-1/M)^s. If the history may depend on the sample, every proper sample admits a deterministic false-completion history. These are exact combinatorial statements, not a bound on every randomized algorithm or on adaptive witness selection.

The full family still has a compact affine description in the truth-table dimension, via lambda(V)=0 and lambda(f)=1. Under a legal gate the family is intersected with one homogeneous hyperplane. If the new gate lies in V it changes nothing; otherwise, for a live coset, there are exactly two cases: if g+V equals f+V then all witnesses are killed and the target becomes available; if the two classes are independent then exactly half survive. This follows from duality in B/V: conditional on lambda(f)=1, the evaluation at an independent class is a balanced linear bit. Thus tracking dimension or number of surviving witnesses is exact, but its unnormalized logarithmic countdown can be exponential even for an easy target, and the terminal gate can kill the entire huge coset at once. The dimension of the ambient truth table is exponential in n. Symbolic representation, query bounds on restricted histories, and state-dependent witness selection are not excluded by the isolated-witness theorem.

## 5. Bounded-history completeness: a contrasting positive theorem

Let d=dim V, f notin V, and t>=0 a charged gate horizon. A fixed subfamily S is t-complete if every legal history of length <=t that leaves f unavailable has at least one surviving member of S. There are two independent upper bounds.

First, parameterize the complete witness coset by lambda_0+sum_i x_i l_i with D=2^n-d-1 independent direction vectors l_i and x in F2^D. A gate sequence of length <=t imposes at most t affine linear equations on x. If f is unavailable the equations are consistent. A consistent rank-r system admits a solution of Hamming weight <=r: select a column basis of the coefficient matrix and express the right-hand side using those columns. Thus choosing all parameter vectors of Hamming weight <=min(t,D) gives a deterministic t-complete family of size sum_{i=0}^{min(t,D)} binomial(D,i). This applies even to arbitrary linear feature additions, not just legal gates. Hamming weight here refers to affine parameter coordinates, not support size of the witness on input points.

Second, for legal gates a much smaller nonconstructive bound follows by counting histories. A history of at most t gates can be padded to exactly t by redundant zero gates. At step j=0,...,t-1, the number of ordered operand pairs is <=2^(2(d+j)), so the total number of histories is <=H_t=2^(2dt+t(t-1)). Each history leaving f unavailable retains at least a 2^(-t) fraction of the original coset, by the exact cut rule. Sample s witnesses independently uniformly from the initial coset, allowing replacement. For each incomplete history the probability of missing its surviving set is <=exp(-s/2^t). A union bound over all histories, including histories that might be chosen after seeing the sample, gives failure probability <=H_t exp(-s/2^t). In particular s>=ceil(2^t((2dt+t(t-1)) ln 2 + ln(1/delta))) suffices for simultaneous t-completeness with probability >=1-delta, for 0<delta<1. Duplicate samples can be discarded. The bound can always be capped by the full coset size using the full family instead. For affine V and logarithmic t it is polynomial in n; for polynomial t it is singly exponential in n. This is existential/randomized selection from an exponentially long truth-table description of each witness, not a polynomial-time construction or evaluation in n. It places no lower bound on the cost of computing f.

These statements clarify the quantifiers in section 4: all-history completeness requires the entire coset, whereas bounded legal histories may be tested by much smaller fixed families. At horizon D=2^n-d-1 all containing hyperplanes are reachable, so the full family is necessary. The exact minimal size between these extremes depends on the incidences of surviving cosets along legal histories. A t-complete family can only constrain histories through t, so applying the previous unrestricted joint-elimination length bound beyond that horizon does not prove target completion. The finite replay below computes exact minima for three-bit targets and tests the deterministic parameter family against every bounded reachable state.

## 7. Boundary and next direction

This proves an exact gate-realizability property for single-witness avoidance and a sharp completeness barrier for every fixed proper witness subfamily, beyond the previous joint-elimination cardinality argument. The symplectic safe-span construction is a tractable shared-label criterion in explicit truth-table coordinates for one linear constraint; the arbitrary multi-constraint case retains the decomposable feasibility obstruction. The theorem does not supply a short target-avoiding history, a lower bound on computing f, or an efficient general SAT decision method. The exact obstruction suggests focusing on bounded-length histories, where omitted witnesses may be unreachable as isolated obstructions, or on compact state-dependent constraints that retain the entire coset without enumerating it. A viable demand estimate must distinguish necessary target computation from arbitrary long irrelevant safe histories. No unrestricted SAT lower bound or certification.
