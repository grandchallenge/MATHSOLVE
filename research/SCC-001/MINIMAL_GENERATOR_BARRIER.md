# SCC-001: exponential minimal-generator birth with a linear-cost target

Status: author-derived theorem and bounded exact regression; exploratory, not certified. Continuation of [target gluing](TARGET_GLUE.md) and [SCC-001](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATHSOLVE/issues/1309). Public chapter: https://grandchallenge.ai/MATH-PROGRAMME/scc/.

## Statement

Use the initial affine state on n=2m+2 Boolean coordinates a,b,x_i,y_i, with m>=1. Let g=ab and
$$
f=g\prod_{i=1}^m(1+x_i y_i).
$$
Adjoin only g. Let R and R' be the old and new Rees algebras, J and J' the target colon ideals, and M=J'/JR'. Write the degree-one symbols of a,b,x_i,y_i as A,B,X_i,Y_i, and t=gz. The variable z records degree, as in TARGET_GLUE.md.

**Theorem.** The graded minimal generator space M/R'_+ M has dimension 1 in degree one, 2^m in degree m+1 and zero in all other grades. Thus its total dimension is exactly 2^m+1. All input coordinates are essential to f; the multiplicative complexity of f is exactly 2m+1 (construct the displayed factors with m+1 multiplications and combine them with m more; the square-free full-degree monomial has degree 2m+2, so the relative-degree bound supplies the matching lower bound).

## Proof

Put h=product_i(1+x_i y_i) and let S be the homogeneous restriction algebra on supp(f), where a=b=1 and each pair belongs to D={(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)}. Both R and R' restrict onto the same S; t restricts to z. More explicitly,
$$
S=\mathbb F_2[z,X_i,Y_i:i=1,\ldots,m]/
(X_i^2-zX_i,Y_i^2-zY_i,X_iY_i).
$$
The normal forms in each grade are z-padded monomials choosing at most one variable per pair. They span by the displayed relations and are independent by the tensor product of the bases 1,x_i,y_i on D. Thus this presentation is exact. Denote by L,L' the respective images of J,J' in S. The image of JR' is L, since R' maps onto S and L is already an ideal.

For a pair, take the alternative restriction basis 1,U=1+x,V=1+y. Its filtered degrees are 0,1,1. Zero extension by the indicator 1+xy gives respectively 1+xy,1+x,1+y, with full Boolean degrees 2,1,1. Tensor products of these basis vectors with exactly k nonconstant choices have restricted degree k, and their zero extensions on the pair coordinates have degree 2m-k. Both degree assertions extend to arbitrary linear combinations by taking the maximum over nonzero coefficients: the leading monomials of equal degree are independent (input basis gives one selected coordinate per chosen pair; output basis gives xy on unchosen pairs and one coordinate on chosen pairs). This provides simultaneous filtered bases, not merely individual degree estimates.

A nonzero restriction vector p of restricted degree at most r belongs to L_r exactly when its zero extension hp, multiplied by ab, has old degree at most r. The latter degree is 2+deg(hp), since a,b are independent coordinates. Every restriction of degree at most r has an old lift of that degree. For L'_r the same restriction criterion applies and the output degree is 1+deg(hp): upper bound from g as a primitive, lower bound from F'_r contained in F_(r+1). Hence the two images have bases consisting of the tensors with
$$
L_r:\quad k\le r,\quad 2m-k+2\le r;
\qquad
L'_r:\quad k\le r,\quad 2m-k+1\le r .
$$
The inequalities imply that L=zL' (identify homogeneous coefficients as functions and use k<=m). In particular L' vanishes below m+1 and has dimension 2^m at grade m+1, whereas L vanishes there. The restriction quotient M -> L'/L is surjective. Thus at least 2^m minimal generators are necessary at grade m+1, since the quotient vanishes at every lower grade.

To prove the matching upper bound, let U_i=z+X_i,V_i=z+Y_i in S. The grade m+1 basis of L' is given by z times all products choosing one of U_i,V_i for each pair. These generate all of L': on a pair U_i V_i+zU_i+zV_i=z^2, and so multiplying and adding two opposite choices produces the same tensor with that pair's factor replaced by z^2, at the next grade. Inductively every tensor with k nonconstant choices appears at its first grade 2m-k+1, and all later copies follow by multiplication by z. The corresponding lifts to J' are z times the products of z+X_i or z+Y_i. Therefore the quotient L'/L requires exactly 2^m minimal generators in degree m+1.

The kernel of R' -> S is generated by A+z,B+z,t+z,X_iY_i: impose those equations on the degree-one polynomial generators of R' and use the presentation of S above. The remaining square relations already hold in R'. The generators A+z,B+z,X_iY_i belong to J because their coefficient functions vanish on supp(f). The sole remaining generator t+z belongs to J', but is not in (JR')_1: J_0=0 and J_1 lies in the old affine space, whereas t+z has a nonaffine coefficient. Thus the kernel of M -> L'/L is generated by the class of t+z, nonzero at grade one. Lifting the 2^m generators of the image and adding that class generates M. The lower bound in grade m+1 and the independent degree-one class prove the claimed exact graded counts.

The minimal homogeneous generator formula uses ordinary graded Nakayama, or direct induction on degree. All rings here are connected finitely generated graded algebras; the modules are bounded below and finitely generated. No specialist theorem about circuit lower bounds beyond the elementary degree inequality is needed.

## Interpretation and limits

One legal gate creates exponentially many minimal generators even after multiplication by inherited relations is factored out. This is not spectator amplification: every variable is essential. It is a tensor-product mechanism involving the actual target support. The generator space in grade m+1 nevertheless has a compact tensor description as the tensor product of m two-dimensional choice spaces. Thus minimality as an R'-module generator does not imply independence of the computational work needed to introduce the generators. The raw count cannot obey a universal polynomial per-gate ceiling. This rejects that proposed capacity measure, not every target-relative invariant, and not P=NP or P!=NP.

The logarithm of a single such count has at most polynomial range: M is supported in grades at most n (indeed z^n belongs to J and the finite graded components of R' have dimension at most 2^n, with no further minimal generators after grade n), so mu(M) <= (n+1)2^n. A logarithmic rescaling alone cannot provide the required superpolynomial initial demand. A viable next direction must distinguish the cost of producing a factorized *family* of relations from the cardinality of a minimal generating set. Tensor factorization alone is not yet a reuse-resistant potential; no general amortized law is claimed.

## Exact finite replay

Run python3 research/SCC-001/minimal_generator_replay.py. It uses full Boolean truth-table linear algebra (not the analytic restricted proxy) to build both filtrations and colon ideals. At each grade it calculates J'_r/(J_r + R'_1 J'_(r-1)), the correct graded minimal-generator quotient since R' is generated in degree one. It independently verifies the restriction dimensions and independence of the explicit witness classes. PASS for m=1,2,3 (n=4,6,8), with complete generator-grade sequences [0,1,2,0,0], [0,1,0,4,0,0,0], [0,1,0,0,8,0,0,0,0]. Proof is author-derived, no Lean or specialist certification. Novelty remains unaudited. The result does not supply an unrestricted superpolynomial lower bound or classical transfer to the SAT promise family.
