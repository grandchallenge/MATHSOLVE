## 1. Two alternating constraints: decomposable spanning

Let E be any finite-dimensional vector space over F2, with two (possibly dependent or zero) alternating bilinear forms alpha,beta, viewed also as linear functionals on the exterior square. Put K=ker(alpha) intersect ker(beta), and let D be the linear span of the decomposable bivectors in K. Then K=D.

Proof by contradiction: choose q in K minus D of minimum alternating-matrix rank 2r. The rank is positive and cannot be two, since rank-two bivectors are decomposable. Write U for the image of q:E*->E, of dimension d=2r>=4; q on U is invertible as a map U*->U. Denote its inverse alternating form by sigma. In coordinates on U write Q for q and S=Q^{-1} for sigma. Restrict alpha,beta to U. All decomposable corrections below live in the exterior square of U.

Lemma: if sigma is not in span(alpha,beta) on U, there exist u,v in U with alpha(u,v)=beta(u,v)=0 and sigma(u,v)=1. Otherwise for every u the functional sigma(u,-) belongs to span(alpha(u,-),beta(u,-)), by annihilator duality. Thus every u lies in the radical of at least one of sigma, sigma+alpha, sigma+beta, sigma+alpha+beta. The first radical is zero. The remaining three forms are nonzero by hypothesis, and each has even rank >=2. Their radicals have cardinality at most 2^(d-2); their union cannot cover U. Contradiction. In particular the chosen u,v are independent.

For p=u wedge v, the matrix is P=uv^T+vu^T. When sigma(u,v)=1, Q+P has rank d-2: the kernel contains Su,Sv, since QSu=u and PSu=u, and likewise for v; rank can fall by at most two. So q+p is a lower-rank member of K in the same coset modulo D, contradiction.

It remains to handle sigma in span(alpha,beta). Every safe p then satisfies sigma(u,v)=0. There are at least (2^d-1)/3 distinct nonzero safe p: for each nonzero u the intersection of the kernels of alpha(u,-),beta(u,-) has dimension at least d-2>=2 and contains u, so it contains some v outside span(u); each two-plane has exactly three nonzero vectors and determines one nonzero wedge over F2. For each such P we have PSP=0 by isotropy, so Q+P is invertible with inverse S+SPS (direct multiplication verifies both identities). This inverse is alternating: the correction is the wedge-form of the covectors Su,Sv. Distinct P give distinct inverses since S is invertible. There are at least five such inverses but at most four forms in span(alpha,beta). Choose a safe p giving an inverse outside that span. Apply the preceding lemma to q+p to obtain a safe p' reducing rank by two. Then q+p+p' is in K minus D at smaller rank, contradiction. This covers all ranks and proves the theorem. The proof uses finite-dimensional alternating linear algebra only; it is not a novelty claim.

## 2. Every codimension-two feature subspace is safely reachable

Let B be the Boolean truth-function algebra on n bits, V any feature space containing 1 and the coordinate functions. Let H be a codimension-two linear subspace of B containing V, given by independent linear functionals lambda,mu. A safe gate is uv with u,v in V and uv in H. Since Boolean squares equal themselves, the product map on the exterior square of E=V/span(1) takes u wedge v to uv+V in F_2(V)/V, where F_j spans products of at most j old features. The two constraints pull back to alternating forms lambda(uv),mu(uv). The spanning theorem proves exactly

$$
V+\mathrm{span}\{uv:u,v\in V,\ uv\in H\}=H\cap F_2(V).
$$

Suppose V is strictly smaller than H and no safe gate enlarges V. Then H intersect F_2(V)=V, so dim F_2(V)/V<=2. The associated graded algebra A=gr_F B is generated by A_1, with squares of positive-degree homogeneous elements zero. If A_3 is nonzero there exist x,y,z in A_1 with xyz nonzero. The three classes xy,xz,yz in A_2 are independent: multiply a putative relation respectively by z,y,x to isolate each coefficient of xyz. Hence dim A_2>=3, contradiction. Therefore A_3=0, all higher grades vanish by degree-one generation, and F_2(V)=B because products of coordinate literals span B. Then H intersect F_2(V)=H, contradicting V<H. A safe nonredundant gate always exists. Repeating reaches H in exactly dim H-dim V gates, which is optimal by dimension. The argument works from every nonlinear starting state containing affine inputs, not just the initial affine space. It does not assert short gate histories in input bit count or give an efficient search algorithm in that count.

## 3. Sharp bounded-horizon consequence for the full witness coset

Fix f outside V, let N=2^n, d0=dim V, D0=N-d0-1, and let W_f(V) be the affine coset of all lambda annihilating V with lambda(f)=1. It has M=2^D0 members. Assume D0>=1. Any distinct pair lambda,mu in the coset is linearly independent; their common kernel H contains V and avoids f. By section 2 it is safely reachable in exactly D0-1 gates. At H the target-disagreeing witness coset is exactly the chosen pair, since H^perp=span(lambda,mu) and their sum evaluates f to zero.

Consequently the exact minimum cardinality of a fixed witness subfamily that detects every incomplete history of length <=D0-1 is M-1. Necessity: omitting any two members fails at the state just constructed. Sufficiency: every incomplete state within that horizon has dimension <=d0+D0-1=N-2, hence at least two surviving target witnesses; any subfamily omitting just one intersects them. This strengthens the isolated-witness theorem's exact M requirement at horizon D0. These horizons are generally exponential in n; this is a completeness theorem, NOT a lower bound on the cost of constructing f. For D0=1 it says one witness suffices at horizon zero, as expected.

## 4. Three-constraint boundary is real (local spanning only)

In E=F2^4 use ordered exterior coordinates ab,ac,ad,bc,bd,cd. Impose q_ab=0, q_ad=q_bc and q_ac+q_ad+q_bd=0. These are three independent linear equations. Write the allowed matrix coordinates as (0,x,y,y,x+y,z). Its Pfaffian over F2 is x(x+y)+y^2=x+y+xy, vanishing iff x=y=0. A four-by-four alternating matrix has rank <=2 iff its Pfaffian vanishes: the determinant is the square of this Pfaffian, and rank is even. Thus the allowed space has dimension three but its decomposable span has dimension one, generated by c wedge d. At an affine four-input state these restrictions can be realized by linear functionals on the quadratic coefficients of actual Boolean functions. The three-constraint spanning law therefore fails even on a genuine initial feature space. This is a failure of the local safe-span identity, NOT proof that the common kernel of three full Boolean-function witnesses is unreachable after more gates. Whether universal codimension-three safe reachability holds requires a separate argument. Nor is the counterexample a necessary constraint family for SAT.

The two-constraint theorem strengthens the global fixed-coset obstruction and identifies the sharp local threshold where decomposable directions fail to span a linear admissibility space. The next substantive question is whether the three-constraint failure persists under nonlinear extension, or can always be repaired by a safe sequence; distinguish local rank obstruction from persistent global capacity. In particular establish actual short-horizon obstructions for explicit targets, not merely late-horizon exact completeness.
