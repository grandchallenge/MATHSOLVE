## Proved statements

Define Agree d a b as equality of tree shape down to depth d, ignoring all descendants beyond d. For every original Eval e p r t derivation, EnvAgree t e e' implies Eval e' p r t (same output and exact cost). This is induction over all original constructors, including loop_step. The result follows because an in-place elimination loses at most one level and a copying read of a tree of size at most t cannot hide a difference below level t.

A stronger counted-copy invariant is eval_copy_budget. Let H(p) be the number of program constructor occurrences (ignoring literal data sizes), at least one. For L>=3 and an arbitrary run of cost t, there is q with q L<=t, counting reads whose charged cost is at least L, such that initial depth agreement D>=L+H(p)(q+1) implies the same program terminates on the other environment and its outputs agree through D-H(p)q levels. If q=0 the outputs are identical. Its proof is induction over the actual Eval derivation. A single descent loses at most one level; a zero-count subrun returns identical data; a positive-count descent is absorbed by the extra constructor in H. Sequential composition adds counts and subtracts their respective depth allowances. In a continuing loop, matching flag shape and the tail-agreement lemma ensure the new loop-state environment retains the charged invariant. The recursive induction uses the SAME program size, so it imposes no bound on dynamic loop iterations.

The theorem different_runs_copy_lower proves: for any p and two runs on a,b giving different r,s, if Agree (2m) a b, m>=3 and size(r)<=m, then m*m < H(p)*(t+m). The symmetric statement follows by swapping runs if the other output also has size <=m. Proof: if 2m>=m+H(p)(q+1), the invariant and size(r)<=m force equality of results; determinism contradicts their difference. Thus m<H(p)(q+1), multiply by m and use q*m<=t. ## Scope and next mathematical obligation

This lower bound depends critically on charged copying of source data, list-shaped input, and a fixed program description. It is NOT a lower bound for every standard multitape TM, or for nonuniform programs whose descriptions grow with input length. Constant-support formulas suffice, so this is an input-access/model-cost baseline rather than the intrinsic computational difficulty of SAT. It does not exclude any polynomial-time solver of degree two or higher. In particular, no P versus NP claim follows. A future proof must exhibit genuine semantic difficulty for unrestricted uniform classical programs after accounting for this overhead, not merely longer prefixes, more variables, larger game descriptions or restricted strategy/proof systems.
