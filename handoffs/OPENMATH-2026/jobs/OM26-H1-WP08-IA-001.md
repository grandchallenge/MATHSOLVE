## Derivation

A. Triangle sides and the use-2 endpoint restriction.

If a side of a triangular cell contained an arrangement vertex in its relative interior, another arrangement line through that vertex would cross the side transversely and enter the cell interior, contradicting that the region is a cell. Hence every triangle side is one elementary segment.

Let AB be an elementary segment on support line L and suppose both endpoints are ordinary. Let M be the unique non-L line through A and N the unique non-L line through B. Any triangular cell using AB must use M and N as its other two support lines, so its third vertex is the unique point M intersect N. That point lies in only one of the two open half-planes bounded by L. Therefore AB cannot bound triangular cells on both sides of L and cannot have triangle-use 2. Thus every use-2 segment has at least one multiple endpoint.

B. Antipodal fan lemma and local bounds.

At an r-fold point v, label the 2r rays rho_0,...,rho_{2r-1} cyclically. Suppose rho_1,...,rho_{r-1} are consecutive D1 rays. Since each D1 segment has triangle-use 2, each of the r sectors between rho_0 and rho_r is triangular. Let P_i be the far endpoint of the elementary radial segment on rho_i. For 1 <= i <= r-1, P_i is ordinary.

Across the D1 edge vP_i, the two adjacent triangles have third-side support lines through P_i. Because P_i is ordinary, there is only one non-radial arrangement line through P_i, so those two third-side support lines coincide. Inducting over i shows that P_0,P_1,...,P_r all lie on one common transverse arrangement line. But rho_0 and rho_r are antipodal rays of one arrangement line through v, so P_0,v,P_r are collinear. The common transverse line through P_0 and P_r would then also pass through v, making the supposed triangular sectors degenerate. Contradiction. Hence no r-1 consecutive D1 rays exist.

Count the 2r cyclic windows of length r-1. Each contains at most r-2 D1 rays, and each D1 ray occurs in exactly r-1 windows. Therefore
(r-1)d1 <= 2r(r-2),
which gives the integer bound d1 <= 2r-3.

If equality d1=2r-3 held, exactly three rays would not be D1. If all three were isolated, each would be flanked by D1 rays. Both adjacent sectors would then be triangular, so each such non-D1 radial segment would itself have triangle-use 2; because it is not D1, it must be D2. Hence d2 >= 3. If the three non-D1 rays are not all isolated, at least two are consecutive, leaving at most two D1 blocks containing 2r-3 rays in total. One block then has length at least ceil((2r-3)/2)=r-1, contradicting the antipodal fan lemma. Therefore d2 <= 1 implies d1 <= 2r-4.

For r=3 and d1=3, the no-two-consecutive-D1 condition forces alternating D1 and non-D1 rays. Each non-D1 ray is flanked by D1 rays and is therefore D2, so d2=3. For a D1 edge vP between neighboring D2 edges vA and vB, the two adjacent triangles have third sides AP and PB. Since P is ordinary, these are the same arrangement line, so A,P,B are collinear. A and B lie in opposite open half-planes bounded by the D1 support line vP, hence on opposite sides of P along their common transverse line; therefore P lies strictly between A and B.

C. Clean-line parity lemma.

Let L be clean. Because the arrangement is pairwise nonparallel, L has m=n-1 ordinary crossings, and since n is even, m is odd. Order the crossings along L. For each interval i on L, let x_i and y_i record whether the adjacent upper and lower cells are triangular. The two unbounded rays give x_0=y_0=x_m=y_m=0. For every bounded interval, x_i+y_i <= 1 by part A, because otherwise that elementary segment of L would have use 2 with two ordinary endpoints.

At crossing i, let a_i and b_i be the triangle-use counts of the adjacent upper and lower elementary pieces of the transverse line. Then
a_i=x_{i-1}+x_i,
b_i=y_{i-1}+y_i.

Assume L has no crossing with a bounded transverse piece of use 0 or 2. A piece with positive triangle-use is necessarily bounded, and under the assumption every bounded adjacent transverse piece has use exactly 1. Thus a_i,b_i are each 0 or 1, with value 1 exactly when that adjacent transverse piece is bounded. At least one of the two transverse directions is bounded, because that transverse arrangement line has another intersection away from L. Hence (a_i,b_i) is never (0,0).

Let P,Q,R count crossings of types (1,0),(0,1),(1,1). Since
