## 1. Purpose

RH-R032 established a design gate: a viable fixed self-adjoint discrete spectral model must generate the extra logarithm in the Riemann–von Mangoldt counting law. A fixed finite logarithmic interval cannot do this.

RH-R033 records a known positive control. Berry and Keating's 2011 symmetric model supplies one fixed self-adjoint discrete operator family whose smooth counting function reproduces the first two large-\(t\) Riemann counting terms under the source's normalization.

The same source also identifies why that success is insufficient: the later smooth terms disagree and the model lacks the prime-labelled periodic-orbit structure associated with the oscillatory zeta term.

This package does not re-prove the source's operator-theoretic analysis. It records exactly which parts are imported and proves only the campaign comparison and resulting design consequence.

## 3. Imported source results

### 3.1 Fixed self-adjoint discrete realization

For each fixed real extension angle \(\alpha\), the source gives a self-adjoint realization with discrete real spectrum.

This clears the structural gates exposed by RH-R030 and RH-R031:

- the spectral range is unbounded;
- the spectrum is discrete rather than purely continuous.

### 3.2 Fixed-geometry source of the logarithm

The source's classical phase-space area has large-\(E\) behavior

\[
A(E)
=
E(\log E-1)
-
\frac{4\log E}{E}
+\cdots.
\]

The \(E\log E\) term is generated by one fixed Hamiltonian's phase-space geometry. It is not produced by changing the operator with the energy cutoff.

### 3.3 Smooth counting comparison

Under the source's Riemann normalization, the smoothed Riemann counting function is written

\[
N_R^{\mathrm{sm}}(t)
=
\frac{t}{2\pi}
\left(
\log\frac{t}{2\pi}-1
\right)
+\frac78+\cdots.
\]

For the \(\alpha=0\) model, the source obtains

\[
N_H(t)
=
\frac{t}{2\pi}
\left(
\log\frac{t}{2\pi}-1
\right)
-
\frac{8\pi}{t}\log\frac{t}{2\pi}
+\cdots.
\]

Thus the two functions share the first two terms

\[
\frac{t}{2\pi}\log\frac{t}{2\pi}
-
\frac{t}{2\pi}.
\]

The source explicitly does not claim equality beyond this level.

### 3.4 Arithmetic fluctuation mismatch

The source records a structural difference between its classical periodic dynamics and the zeta explicit-formula analogy.

- The symmetric model has one primitive periodic orbit for each energy.
- The zeta spectral analogy requires a prime-labelled family, with periods \(\log p\).

The source gives no mechanism that supplies this missing prime structure.

## 4. Exact theorem-development result

### Theorem RH-R033-SYMMETRIC-BK-TLOGT-001

The protected Berry–Keating 2011 source class is an explicit fixed-operator positive control for the RH-001 smooth-counting gate:

1. there exists a fixed self-adjoint discrete operator family whose source-derived smooth spectral count contains the same leading \(t\log t\) term as the smoothed Riemann-zero count;
2. under the source's \(\alpha=0\), \(E=t/(2\pi)\), \(\eta=1/(2\pi)\) normalization, it also contains the same next linear term \(-t/(2\pi)\);
3. therefore the RH-R032 failure of fixed finite intervals is not a universal obstruction to fixed self-adjoint discrete operator models;
4. agreement through these two smooth terms is nevertheless insufficient for an exact Hilbert–Pólya realization, because the source's subsequent smooth terms differ and the prime-labelled fluctuation mechanism is absent.

This theorem is a governed comparison of already published source results. It is not a new spectral theorem and does not imply RH.

## 5. Proof of the campaign comparison

Define the shared leading pair

\[
M(t)
=
\frac{t}{2\pi}
\left(
\log\frac{t}{2\pi}-1
\right).
\]

The protected source gives

\[
N_R^{\mathrm{sm}}(t)=M(t)+\frac78+\cdots
\]

and

\[
N_H(t)
=
M(t)
-
\frac{8\pi}{t}\log\frac{t}{2\pi}
+\cdots.
\]

Hence both source expansions contain exactly the same \(t\log t\) and linear-\(t\) terms. This proves claims 1 and 2.

RH-R032 showed that every fixed finite-interval Berry–Keating realization has

\[
N_{\mathrm{finite}}(t)
=
c\,t+O(1)
\]

for a fixed constant \(c\), and therefore cannot generate the logarithmic enhancement. The present source model does generate that enhancement while remaining fixed. This proves claim 3.

For claim 4, the displayed source expansions are already unequal after the shared leading pair. More importantly, the source's own periodic-orbit analysis identifies no prime-indexed orbit family corresponding to the oscillatory arithmetic contribution. Therefore equality through the leading smooth pair cannot be promoted to exact spectral correspondence.

QED.

## 7. False-proof firewall

Rejected moves:

1. Matching \(t\log t\) and the next linear term does not imply equality of counting functions.
2. Matching the smooth count does not imply matching the oscillatory arithmetic term.
3. A self-adjoint real spectrum does not constrain \(\Re(\rho)\) without an independently proved zero-to-eigenvalue correspondence.
4. The extension parameter \(\alpha\) may not be chosen separately for each level.
5. Numerical level proximity may not replace the missing prime-labelled structure.
6. “Compact hamiltonian” in the source title may not be read as a bounded-operator claim; the audited spectrum is unbounded and therefore does not contradict RH-R030.
7. The source's model may not be represented as a solution of the Hilbert–Pólya problem; the authors explicitly state the remaining mismatch.

## 8. Exact design lesson

The campaign frontier has moved.

Before RH-R033, a live question was:

> Can one fixed self-adjoint discrete operator generate \(T\log T\) spectral counting?

The protected source answers that design question positively in a non-zeta model.

The next question is narrower and materially harder:

> What fixed self-adjoint spectral mechanism can generate the arithmetic fluctuation / prime-labelled orbit content while preserving the already-correct smooth \(T\log T-t\) count?

Any next candidate must be rejected if it obtains prime structure merely by inserting the known zeta zeros as spectral data or by changing the operator with the spectral cutoff.
