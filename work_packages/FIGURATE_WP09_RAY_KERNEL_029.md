# FIGURATE WP09-029 — finite oriented ray kernel and two full base-case class groups

Parent: [FIGURATE WP09 #3145](https://github.com/grandchallenge/MATH-PROGRAMME/issues/3145). This producer theorem computes the finite ray **kernel** for the actual potentially nonmaximal order, for every specified $m$ subject to a strict computational guard. It also proves the **full** oriented ray class groups at $m=0,1$. It does NOT compute the narrow Picard group or full ray group at general $m$, classify all successful anchor parameters, or assert independent MATHCERT certification.

## Theorem A — uniform dual-number residue group

For $m\ge0$ let $A=8m+1$, $D=2A$, and $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\sqrt D]$. The integral ray modulus is $A\mathcal O$, not $A\mathcal O_K$ when $\mathcal O$ is nonmaximal. Because $D=2A$, the residue $\epsilon=\sqrt D\bmod A\mathcal O$ obeys $\epsilon^2=0$, so

$$
\mathcal O/A\mathcal O\cong(\mathbb Z/A)[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2).
$$

A residue $x+y\epsilon$ is a unit exactly when $\gcd(x,A)=1$. The map

$$
x+y\epsilon\mapsto(x,yx^{-1})
$$

is a group isomorphism from $(\mathcal O/A\mathcal O)^\times$ to the direct product $(\mathbb Z/A)^\times\times(\mathbb Z/A,+)$. Its multiplication is $(x,t)(u,v)=(xu,t+v)$, and it has order $A\varphi(A)$. The $A=1$ case is the trivial group. **Proof:** the nilpotent term has square zero, and $(x+y\epsilon)(u+v\epsilon)=xu+(xv+yu)\epsilon$; dividing the second coefficient by $xu$ gives $t+v$. This uses precisely the nonmaximal-order integral basis when needed. QED.

## Theorem B — complete ray-unit kernel and exact sequence

Let $U+V\sqrt D$ be the minimal positive norm-one Pell unit; it generates the totally positive units of the real quadratic order. Its residue under the preceding isomorphism is $g=(U,VU^{-1})$ modulo $A$. The oriented order ray Picard sequence gives

$$
1\longrightarrow K_A\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Cl}^{+}_{A\mathcal O}(\mathcal O)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}^{+}(\mathcal O)
\longrightarrow1,
$$

where the **entire kernel**, including the conductor-sensitive coefficientwise ray, is

$$
K_A=\frac{(\mathbb Z/A)^\times\times(\mathbb Z/A,+)}
{\langle(U,VU^{-1})\rangle}.
$$

**Proof:** forgetting the ray gives the surjection to narrow ideal classes. Its kernel consists of principal ideals modulo totally positive ray-principal ideals, hence the residue unit group modulo the image of globally totally positive units. Theorem A identifies this quotient explicitly; the ray stays in $\mathcal O$, not $\mathcal O_K$. QED.

The protected anchor target $\rho=r(1+\sqrt D)$, $r=(A+1)/2$, has residue coordinates $(r,1)$. The quotient is computable **without generalized-Pell norm-representative enumeration**.

## Two complete full ray class groups

**At $m=0$:** $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]$ is norm-Euclidean: independently rounding two coefficients gives absolute remainder norm at most $1/4+2/4=3/4<1$. It has a norm-negative unit $1+\sqrt2$, so its narrow class group is trivial. Since $A=1$, the kernel is trivial as well; the **full oriented ray group is trivial**.

**At $m=1$:** $A=9$, $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[3\sqrt2]$ has conductor three. The maximal order $\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]$ has class number one. The conductor Picard exact sequence identifies the ordinary Picard group of $\mathcal O$ as a quotient of $(\mathcal O_K/3)^\times$ by the images of $(\mathbb Z/3)^\times$ and $\mathcal O_K^\times$. Here $\mathcal O_K/3\cong\mathbb F_9$, whose cyclic unit group has order eight and is generated by the residue of $1+\sqrt2$: its fourth power is $-1$ and its eighth power is $1$. Hence the **ordinary** Picard group of $\mathcal O$ is trivial.

A norm-$-1$ unit in $\mathcal O$ would satisfy $x^2-18y^2=-1$, which modulo three requires a square to equal two, impossible. Therefore its narrow Picard group has order two. The exact ray kernel is $C_3$. The full oriented ray group is abelian and has order six, so

$$
\operatorname{Cl}^{+}_{9\mathcal O}(\mathcal O)\cong C_6.
$$

This is a full class group computation **only for this base case**, not an extrapolation.
