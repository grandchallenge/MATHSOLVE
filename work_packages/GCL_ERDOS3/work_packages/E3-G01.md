# E3-G01 — gluing-defect and extremizer-tree attack

Frontier node: `E3-Q4-DENSITY-LOSS`.

This is the primary theorem-attack WP for the tranche.

## Mission

Convert near-equality in the elementary partition bound into a rigid
near-extremizer tree, then use cross-block (4)-AP constraints to force a
quantitative density loss.

For fixed (L), let (m=2^L) and
[
D_{L,n}:=a_n-a_{n+L}.
]

A (4)-AP-free extremizer (Asubseteq[1,m2^n]) can be partitioned into
(m) consecutive fibers of length (2^n). Each fiber has density at most
(a_n), while their average density is (a_{n+L}).

## Required work

### Part A — exact near-equality lemma

1. Express (D_{L,n}) exactly as the average fiber deficit of a
   scale-(n+L) extremizer relative to (a_n).

2. Iterate the partition for (t) levels and prove the strongest correct
   concentration statement of the form:

   if
   [
   a_n-a_{n+tL}
   ]
   is small, then a quantitatively large family of nodes/paths in the
   (m)-ary partition tree are near-extremal at every or most levels.

3. State all constants. Do not replace average near-extremality by uniform
   near-extremality without proof.

### Part B — local incompatibility

4. Seek a theorem saying that (m) mutually positioned near-extremal
   (4)-AP-free fibers cannot satisfy all cross-fiber progression
   constraints without total deficit at least
   [
   c,a_n^{1+	heta}
   ]
   for some (0<	heta<1).

5. You may derive the required constraints directly or use the digit/carry
   representation in the protected representation note. Do not depend on
   another contributor's unpublished return.

6. If the full one-step inequality is inaccessible, prove a weaker
   persistence statement sufficient for
   [
   P(alpha)llalpha^{-	heta},qquad 	heta<1,
   ]
   or reduce it to one exact finite/local gluing theorem.

## Strong failure is valuable

If near-extremizer trees can coexist with arbitrarily small cross-block
penalty under a concrete construction, return that construction and identify
which part of the promoted route fails.
