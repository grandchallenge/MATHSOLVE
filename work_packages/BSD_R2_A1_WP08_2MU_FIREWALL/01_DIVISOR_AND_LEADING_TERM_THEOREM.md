# Divisor and leading-term theorem

## 1. Algebraic setting

Put

`Lambda = Z_2[[T]]`.

For a nonzero `f in Lambda`, define

`mu(f) = max {m >= 0 : f in 2^m Lambda}`.

Equivalently, if `f=sum a_n T^n`, then

`mu(f)=min_n ord_2(a_n)`.

The height-one prime `(2)` therefore has valuation

`v_(2)(f)=mu(f)`.

We use the standard Weierstrass preparation theorem over the complete DVR `Z_2`: after removing `2^mu(f)`, a nonzero power series factors as a unit times a distinguished polynomial, and the distinguished polynomial has unique irreducible factorization. Hence `Lambda` is a UFD; its height-one primes are generated by irreducible elements.

This paragraph is the only structural commutative-algebra input. All deductions below are explicit factorization arguments.

## Theorem 1 — exact height-one `(2)` defect

Let `f,g` be nonzero elements of `Lambda`. Assume

`v_P(f)=v_P(g)`

for every height-one prime `P != (2)`.

Then there exist a unique integer `delta` and a unit `u in Lambda^x` such that

`f/g = 2^delta u`

in `Frac(Lambda)`. Moreover

`delta = mu(f)-mu(g)`.

### Proof

Factor in the UFD `Lambda`:

`f = u_f 2^a product_i pi_i^(e_i)`

and

`g = u_g 2^b product_i pi_i^(d_i)`,

where each `pi_i` is irreducible and not associated to `2`, and only finitely many exponents are nonzero.

The hypothesis at every height-one prime other than `(2)` says `e_i=d_i` for every `i`. Cancelling those factors gives

`f/g = 2^(a-b) (u_f/u_g)`.

Take `delta=a-b` and `u=u_f/u_g`. Since units have zero valuation at every height-one prime, `delta` is forced by the `(2)` valuation and is unique. By definition `a=mu(f)` and `b=mu(g)`. QED.

## Corollary 2 — equality after inverting 2

For nonzero principal ideals `(f)` and `(g)`,

`(f) Lambda[1/2] = (g) Lambda[1/2]`

if and only if

`f = 2^delta u g`

in `Frac(Lambda)` for some `delta in Z` and `u in Lambda^x`.

Thus localization at `2` erases exactly the relative `(2)` exponent.

### Proof

Inverting `2` makes `2` a unit and leaves every other height-one prime visible. Equality in the localization is therefore equivalent to equality of all non-`(2)` height-one valuations. Apply Theorem 1. The converse is immediate. QED.

## Theorem 3 — one-sided divisibility firewall

Suppose

`v_P(f) >= v_P(g)`

for every height-one prime `P != (2)`.

Then there is a least integer

`m = max(0, mu(g)-mu(f))`

such that

`g | 2^m f`

in `Lambda`.

In particular, a divisibility verified at every height-one prime away from `(2)` can fail integrally only through a missing power of `2`.

### Proof

For every non-`(2)` irreducible factor, the exponent in `f` is at least the exponent in `g` by hypothesis. Multiplication by `2^m` changes no such exponent. At `(2)`, divisibility is equivalent to

`mu(f)+m >= mu(g)`.

The stated `m` is the least nonnegative integer satisfying this inequality. UFD divisibility then proves the claim. QED.

## Theorem 4 — augmentation-leading-term transfer

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem 1, so

`f = 2^delta u g`

in `Frac(Lambda)` with `u in Lambda^x`.

Let `r=ord_T(g)` in `Q_2[[T]]`. Then `ord_T(f)=r`. Write

`g=T^r g_r(T)` and `f=T^r f_r(T)`

with `g_r(0),f_r(0) != 0`. Then

`ord_2(f_r(0)) = ord_2(g_r(0)) + delta`.

### Proof

Neither `2^delta` nor a unit of `Lambda` contains a factor `T`, so the `T`-orders agree. Cancelling `T^r` gives

`f_r(T)=2^delta u(T) g_r(T)`.

A unit in `Z_2[[T]]` has odd constant term, hence `ord_2(u(0))=0`. Evaluate at `T=0` and take `ord_2`. QED.

## Corollary 5 — mu difference shifts a rank-one leading coefficient

If `f` and `g` are characteristic/analytic elements for which an away-from-`(2)` equality has been established and both have augmentation order one, then

`ord_2(first_nonzero_coefficient(f))`

`= ord_2(first_nonzero_coefficient(g)) + mu(f)-mu(g)`.

The same statement holds for any common augmentation order `r`.

This is a conditional algebraic transfer statement. It does not assert that the needed analytic/algebraic equality or the common augmentation order has been proved for `BSD-R2-A1`.
