# WP20 theorem — exact rank-one determinant derivative factorization

## Setup

Let `R` be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field `K` and uniformizer `pi`. Let

`S := R[[T]]`.

Fix an integer `m>=1` and a matrix

`A(T) in M_m(S)`.

Write

`A(T)=A_0 + T A_1 + T^2 A_2 + ...`

with `A_i in M_m(R)`.

Assume

`rank_K(A_0)=m-1`.

Set

`M := coker(A_0:R^m -> R^m)`.

Then `M` is a finitely presented `R`-module of rank one and `Tor_R(M)` is finite.

Because `R^m` is torsion-free, `ker(A_0)` is saturated in `R^m`; hence it is free of rank one. Likewise

`M/Tor_R(M)`

is free of rank one.

## The intrinsic first-order map

For `x in ker(A_0)`, define

`beta_A(x)`

as the image of `A_1 x` in

`M/Tor_R(M)`.

This gives an `R`-linear map

`beta_A : ker(A_0) -> M/Tor_R(M)`.

Both source and target are free rank one. Choose primitive generators `e` and `f` and write

`beta_A(e)=b f`

for a unique `b in R`.

Changing either primitive generator multiplies `b` by a unit. Therefore the principal ideal

`B_A := b R`

is intrinsic.

We call `B_A` the rank-one Bockstein ideal of the first-order deformation `A(T)`.

## Theorem `BSD-A1-WP20-DET-BETA-001`

With the setup above,

`(coeff_T det A(T)) R
 = Fitt^1_R(M) * B_A`.

Equivalently, if

`det A(T)=a_1 T + a_2 T^2 + ...`,

then

`a_1 R = Fitt^1_R(M) B_A`.

### Proof

By Smith normal form over the DVR `R`, there exist

`P,Q in GL_m(R)`

such that

`P A_0 Q = D := diag(d_1,...,d_{m-1},0)`

with each `d_i` nonzero.

Define

`C(T):=P A(T) Q`.

Then

`C(T)=D + T C_1 + O(T^2)`

with

`C_1=P A_1 Q`.

Since `det(P)det(Q)` is a unit,

`det C(T)=u det A(T)`

for some `u in R^x`.

Thus the principal ideals generated by the coefficients of `T` in the two determinants are equal.

For the diagonal specialization `D`, the coefficient of `T` in `det C(T)` is obtained by differentiating the determinant at `D`. Every cofactor of `D` vanishes except the `(m,m)` cofactor. Hence

`coeff_T det C(T)
 = (d_1 ... d_{m-1}) (C_1)_{mm}`.

Now compute the two intrinsic factors.

First, `coker(D)` is

`R/(d_1) direct_sum ... direct_sum R/(d_{m-1}) direct_sum R`.

Therefore

`Fitt^1_R(coker D)=(d_1 ... d_{m-1})`.

Fitting ideals are invariant under invertible changes of presentation, so

`Fitt^1_R(M)=(d_1 ... d_{m-1})`.

Second, in the diagonal presentation

`ker(D)=R e_m`

and the free quotient of `coker(D)` is generated by the class of `e_m`. The induced first-order map sends

`e_m |-> C_1 e_m mod im(D)`,

whose free component is exactly

`(C_1)_{mm}`.

Thus the Bockstein ideal is

`B_A=((C_1)_{mm})`.

Combining the two identities gives

`(coeff_T det A(T))
 = Fitt^1_R(M) B_A`.

This proves the theorem. QED.

## Edge case `m=1`

If `m=1`, the rank hypothesis forces `A_0=0`. Then `M=R`,

`Fitt^1_R(R)=R`

by the standard convention that the ideal generated by `0 x 0` minors is `R`. Also

`beta_A:R->R`

is multiplication by the coefficient `A_1`, and

`det A(T)=A_1 T+O(T^2)`.

So the theorem gives

`(A_1)=R*(A_1)`

exactly.

## Torsion-free specialization

If `Tor_R(M)=0`, then every nonzero Smith factor `d_i` is a unit. Hence

`Fitt^1_R(M)=R`.

The theorem becomes

`(coeff_T det A(T))=B_A`.

Thus the entire first derivative is Bockstein in the torsion-free rank-one case.

## Valuation corollary `BSD-A1-WP20-VAL-001`

Assume `a_1 != 0`. Then

`v_pi(a_1)
 = v_pi(Fitt^1_R(M)) + v_pi(B_A)`.

For `R=Z_2`, protected WP19 identifies

`v_2(Fitt^1_{Z_2}(X_E))
 = len_{Z_2} Tor(X_E)`.

Therefore any arithmetic determinant presentation specializing exactly to `X_E` satisfies

`ord_2(first determinant derivative)
 = len_{Z_2} Tor(X_E) + v_2(B_A)`.

No arithmetic interpretation of `B_A` is asserted by this corollary.

## Invariance under power-series basis changes

Let

`U(T),V(T) in GL_m(R[[T]])`

and define

`A'(T):=U(T)A(T)V(T)`.

Then the specialization modules are canonically isomorphic up to the induced invertible bases, and

`det A'(T)=det U(T) det V(T) det A(T)`.

Because `det A(0)=0`, the coefficient of `T` in `det A'(T)` is the coefficient of `T` in `det A(T)` multiplied by the unit

`det U(0) det V(0)`.

For the induced Bockstein map, the extra derivative terms are

`U_1 A_0 V_0 + U_0 A_0 V_1`.

On `ker(A_0)`, the `A_0 V_1` contribution lands in `im(A_0)`, and the `U_1 A_0` contribution vanishes after applying `A_0` to the kernel input. Hence, after the natural identifications of kernel and free cokernel, the Bockstein scalar changes only by units from the chosen primitive bases.

Thus both sides of the ideal identity are presentation-invariant under invertible `R[[T]]` basis changes.

## Adjugate formulation

The same theorem can be written without choosing Smith coordinates. Jacobi's formula at the singular specialization gives

`coeff_T det A(T)=Tr(adj(A_0) A_1)`.

Because `rank_K(A_0)=m-1`, `adj(A_0)` has rank one over `K`. The ideal generated by all entries of `adj(A_0)` is exactly

`Fitt^1_R(M)`.

The theorem identifies the residual scalar after removing this common first-Fitting factor with the intrinsic Bockstein map

`ker(A_0) -> M/Tor(M)`.

## Claim boundary

This theorem is pure commutative algebra. It does not assert that an arithmetic Selmer complex admits such a square presentation with determinant equal to a Mazur-Tate, Kato, p-adic-L, or complex-L analytic element. It does not identify `B_A` with any regulator or local factor. Those are the remaining arithmetic obligations.
