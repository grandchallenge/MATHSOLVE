# Route-barrier theorem

## Proposition `BSD-A1-HB001`

Let the selected BSD class satisfy the protected `BSD-R2-A1` hypotheses. From the currently admitted Heegner-primitivity source interfaces, there is no valid mechanical specialization of the standard odd-prime proof chain

`rank-one Selmer`

`-> odd-prime level raising / rank lowering`

`-> primitive Heegner Kolyvagin system`

`-> sharp Sha / Heegner-index equality`

from odd `p` to `p=2`.

**Status:** `PROVED_FROM_ADMITTED_SOURCE_INTERFACES`.

## Proof

The admitted MATHFORGE audit isolates two primary-source facts.

### 1. The sharp index theorem screened is odd-prime only

Burungale–Castella–Kim work in a good-ordinary setup with `p>3`. Under their hypotheses, primitivity of the Heegner Kolyvagin system upgrades Kolyvagin's structure theorem to the exact equality

`ord_p(#Sha(E/K)[p^infinity]) = 2 ord_p([E(K): Z y_K])`.

This source therefore identifies the exact mechanism needed by the direct arithmetic lane, but its theorem does not include `p=2`.

### 2. The odd-prime rank-lowering step has a real mod-2 failure

The odd-prime rank-lowering input used in the standard primitivity strategy requires a level-raising prime satisfying a congruence condition of the form

`q != +/-1 (mod p)`.

At `p=2`, every odd `q` is congruent to `+/-1` modulo `2`, so that condition cannot be reproduced.

Chao Li goes further than this formal congruence observation. Under the stronger residual hypotheses of his mod-2 Heegner setting, including surjectivity, his rank-one case satisfies

`s_2(E/K)=1  =>  s_lambda(A/K)=2`

for the relevant level-raising operation. Thus the analogue does not lower Selmer rank from one to zero in that source class.

Together, these facts rule out the mechanical substitution `p=2` in the standard odd-prime primitivity proof technology.

## Lemma `BSD-A1-HB005` — residual image dichotomy

If `E[2]` is irreducible as an `F_2[G_Q]`-module, then the image of

`rho_bar_{E,2}: G_Q -> GL_2(F_2)`

is either the order-three subgroup `C_3` or all of `GL_2(F_2) ~= S_3`.

**Status:** `PROVED_IN_PACKAGE`.

### Proof

`GL_2(F_2)` has order six and is isomorphic to `S_3`. Its proper subgroup orders are `1`, `2`, and `3`.

A trivial subgroup fixes every line in `F_2^2`. Any order-two subgroup is generated by a nonidentity element of order two; in characteristic two such an element has eigenvalue `1` and therefore fixes a nonzero vector, hence a one-dimensional subspace. Either case makes the representation reducible.

The order-three subgroup cyclically permutes the three nonzero vectors of `F_2^2` and fixes no one-dimensional subspace, so it acts irreducibly. The full group also acts irreducibly.

Thus irreducibility leaves exactly the `C_3` and `S_3` images. QED.

## Scope firewall

The route-barrier proposition is deliberately weaker than a p=2 no-go theorem.

Chao Li's source uses the surjective residual case, hence the `S_3` branch of `BSD-A1-HB005`, together with additional local hypotheses. It does not prove the same rank-lowering obstruction for the `C_3` branch, nor for every selected curve in the `S_3` branch absent those additional hypotheses.

Nothing here excludes:

- a different p=2 primitivity proof;
- a sharp p=2 index theorem proved without the obstructed rank-lowering step;
- a direct arithmetic-length theorem over `Q`;
- an integral Iwasawa argument.

## Consequence

The next direct proof step cannot be described as "apply the known Heegner primitivity theorem at 2." A uniform proof for `BSD-R2-A1` must handle both residual-image branches and requires a materially new integral p=2 theorem or a different exact arithmetic bridge.
