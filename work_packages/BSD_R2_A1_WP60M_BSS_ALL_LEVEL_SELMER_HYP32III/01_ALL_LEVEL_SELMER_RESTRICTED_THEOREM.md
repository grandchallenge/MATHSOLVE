## 2. Group-cohomology theorem for the natural module

Let

`V_m:=R_m^2`,

`W_m:=V_m[2]=2^(m-1)V_m ~= F_2^2`.

The action on `W_m` factors through

`G_1=GL_2(F_2)~=S3`.

### Lemma `BSD-A1-WP60M-NORMALIZE-001`

The inclusion `W_m -> V_m` induces an isomorphism

`H^1(G_m,W_m) -> H^1(G_m,V_m)`.

### Proof

First, `V_m/W_m ~= (Z/2^(m-1))^2` has no `G_m`-invariants. Indeed, the residual matrices include the standard upper transvection and a matrix interchanging the two coordinate axes; their common fixed submodule is zero. The long exact sequence attached to

`0 -> W_m -> V_m -> V_m/W_m -> 0`

therefore makes

`H^1(G_m,W_m) -> H^1(G_m,V_m)`

injective.

For surjectivity, let `c:G_m->V_m` be a 1-cocycle and let `z=-I`, which is central. Comparing `c(zg)` and `c(gz)` gives

`2c(g)=(1-g)c(z)`

for every `g`.

Reduce modulo `2`. The left side vanishes, so `c(z) mod 2` is fixed by `G_1`. The natural `F_2^2` module has no `G_1`-invariants. Hence `c(z)` is even: write

`c(z)=2v`.

Replace `c` by the cohomologous cocycle `c+delta_v`. Since

`delta_v(z)=(-I-1)v=-2v`,

the new cocycle has `c(z)=0`. The displayed central relation now gives

`2c(g)=0`

for all `g`. Thus the normalized cocycle takes values in `W_m`.

Every cohomology class therefore lies in the image. QED.

### 2.1 Residual cohomology of `G_m`

Let

`P_m:=ker(G_m -> G_1)`.

Because `P_m` acts trivially on `W_m`, inflation-restriction gives

`0 -> H^1(G_1,W_m)
   -> H^1(G_m,W_m)
   -> Hom(P_m,W_m)^(G_1)`.

The first term is zero. To see this, use the normal `C3=A3` in `S3`: the order-three element has no fixed vector on the natural two-dimensional module, and `3` is invertible in `F_2`; hence both `W_m^C3` and `H^1(C3,W_m)` vanish, forcing `H^1(S3,W_m)=0`.

It remains to bound the equivariant Hom space.

For `r>=1`, define

`P_m(r):=ker(G_m -> GL_2(Z/2^r))`.

Thus `P_m(1)=P_m`, `P_m(m)=1`, and

`P_m(r)/P_m(r+1) ~= M_2(F_2)`

with `G_1` acting by conjugation.

### Lemma `BSD-A1-WP60M-CONGRUENCE-HOM-002`

`Hom(P_m,W_m)^(G_1)` has dimension at most `1` over `F_2`.

### Proof

Let `phi:P_m->W_m` be a `G_1`-equivariant homomorphism.

Since `W_m` has exponent two, `phi` kills squares.

For every `r>=2`, squaring satisfies

`(I+2^r A)^2
 = I+2^(r+1)A mod P_m(r+2)`.

Starting at the top of the congruence filtration and descending, this shows

`phi(P_m(3))=0`.

Now examine `P_m(2)/P_m(3)`. For `A in M_2(F_2)`,

`(I+2A)^2
 = I+4(A+A^2) mod P_m(3)`.

The vectors `A+A^2` span the trace-zero hyperplane of `M_2(F_2)`. Hence `phi` on `P_m(2)/P_m(3)` can factor only through the trace quotient

`M_2(F_2)/sl_2(F_2) ~= F_2`.

This quotient is trivial under conjugation. Since `W_m^(G_1)=0`, equivariance forces the induced map to vanish. Thus

`phi(P_m(2))=0`.

Therefore `phi` factors through

`P_m/P_m(2) ~= M_2(F_2)`.

An elementary equivariance calculation with

`U=[[1,1],[0,1]]`, `S=[[0,1],[1,0]]`

shows

`dim_F2 Hom_(G_1)(M_2(F_2),F_2^2)=1`.

Explicitly, up to scalar the unique nonzero equivariant map is

`psi([[a,b],[c,d]])=(a+b+d, a+c+d)`.

Hence the original equivariant Hom space has dimension at most one. QED.

### 2.2 Existence of the nonzero class

Protected WP60K gives an even-valued cocycle

`z_4:G_2 -> (Z/4)^2`

representing the nonzero class, with

`z_4(U)=(0,2)`.

Divide its values by `2` and reduce modulo `2` to obtain a nonzero cocycle

`bar z:G_2 -> F_2^2`.

For every `m>=2`, compose reduction `G_m->G_2` with `bar z`, and identify `F_2^2` with `W_m` by multiplication by `2^(m-1)`. This gives a cocycle

`z_m:G_m->W_m subset V_m`.

At the primitive upper unipotent,

`z_m(U)=(0,2^(m-1))`.

A coboundary on the cyclic subgroup generated by `U` has value

`(U-1)(x,y)=(y,0)`.

Therefore `z_m|_<U>` is nonzero in cohomology. In particular `z_m` is not globally a coboundary.

Combining existence with Lemmas `NORMALIZE-001` and `CONGRUENCE-HOM-002` gives:

### Theorem `BSD-A1-WP60M-H1-003`

For every `m>=2`,

`H^1(GL_2(Z/2^m),(Z/2^m)^2) ~= Z/2`.

Its unique nonzero class is represented by

`z_m(g)=2^(m-2) z_4(g mod 4) mod 2^m`.

It restricts nontrivially to the primitive upper-unipotent subgroup.

QED.

## 3. Exact BSS finite auxiliary field at every level

For `R=Z/2^m`, the BSS annihilator power is `M=2^m`. Over `Q`, the Hilbert-class contribution is trivial and the unit group is `{+-1}`. Therefore

`K_(2^m)=Q(mu_(2^(m+1)))`.

Put

`L_m:=Q(E[2^m])`.

The Weil pairing gives

`Q(mu_(2^m)) subset L_m`.

### Lemma `BSD-A1-WP60M-AUX-FIELD-004`

`L_m intersect Q(mu_(2^(m+1)))=Q(mu_(2^m))`.

### Proof

Full selected 2-adic image supplies a Galois element with matrix

`diag(1+2^m,1)`.

This matrix is the identity modulo `2^m`, so the element fixes `L_m`. Its determinant is `1+2^m mod 2^(m+1)`, the nontrivial element of

`Gal(Q(mu_(2^(m+1)))/Q(mu_(2^m)))`.

Thus it does not fix `Q(mu_(2^(m+1)))`. Since the latter extension over `Q(mu_(2^m))` has degree two, the intersection is exactly `Q(mu_(2^m))`. QED.

Hence, with

`F_m:=K(A_m)_(2^m)=L_m Q(mu_(2^(m+1)))`,

one has

`[F_m:L_m]=2`.

Let

`Gamma_m:=Gal(F_m/Q)`

and

`N_m:=Gal(F_m/L_m)~=C2`.

The order-two normal subgroup `N_m` is central and acts trivially on `A_m`.

Inflation-restriction gives

`0 -> H^1(G_m,A_m)
   -> H^1(Gamma_m,A_m)
   -> H^1(N_m,A_m)^(G_m)`.

Since

`H^1(N_m,A_m)=Hom(C2,A_m)=A_m[2]=W_m`

and `W_m^(G_m)=0`, the right term vanishes.

### Theorem `BSD-A1-WP60M-AUX-H1-005`

For every `m>=2`, inflation is an isomorphism

`H^1(G_m,A_m) ~= H^1(F_m/Q,A_m)`.

Consequently

`#H^1(K(E[2^m])_(2^m)/Q,E[2^m])=2`.

Under this isomorphism the unique nonzero class is `z_m`.
