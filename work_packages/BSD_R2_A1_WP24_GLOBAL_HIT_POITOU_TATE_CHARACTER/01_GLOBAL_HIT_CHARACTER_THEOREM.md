## 2. Compact primitive Selmer group

Let

`S_T(Q)`

be the compact self-dual Selmer group for `T` with the local conditions orthogonal to the primitive classical Kummer conditions for `A`.

Equivalently, it is the inverse limit of the finite primitive Kummer Selmer groups under the transition maps induced by

`[2]:E[2^(n+1)] -> E[2^n]`.

### Lemma `BSD-A1-WP24-COMPACT-SELMER-001`

There is a canonical isomorphism

`S_T(Q) ~= E(Q) tensor Z_2`.

Consequently

`S_T(Q) ~= Z_2`.

### Proof

Take inverse limits of the protected finite-level Kummer exact sequences

`0 -> E(Q)/2^nE(Q)
   -> Sel_{2^n}(E/Q)
   -> Sha(E/Q)[2^n]
   -> 0`.

On the Mordell-Weil term the transition maps are the natural reductions, and

`lim_n E(Q)/2^nE(Q) = E(Q) tensor Z_2`

because `E(Q)` is finitely generated.

On the Sha term the transition map is multiplication by `2`, so

`lim_n Sha(E/Q)[2^n] = T_2 Sha(E/Q)`.

Protected finiteness of `Sha(E/Q)` gives

`T_2 Sha(E/Q)=0`:

a nonzero compatible sequence would produce elements of arbitrarily large `2`-power order in the finite group `Sha(E/Q)[2^infinity]`.

Inverse limits are left exact. Since the right-hand inverse limit is zero, the natural injection

`E(Q) tensor Z_2 -> S_T(Q)`

is also surjective. Thus

`S_T(Q) ~= E(Q) tensor Z_2`.

Finally,

`E(Q) ~= Z direct_sum E(Q)_tors`

with `E(Q)_tors` of odd order. Tensoring with `Z_2` kills the finite odd-order summand, so

`E(Q) tensor Z_2 ~= Z_2`. QED.

## 3. A saturated generator and its localization

Choose a point

`P in E(Q)`

whose image generates the free quotient

`E(Q)/E(Q)_tors ~= Z`.

Let

`t_P in S_T(Q)`

be the corresponding compact Kummer class. By the preceding lemma, `t_P` is a `Z_2`-basis of `S_T(Q)`.

### Lemma `BSD-A1-WP24-LOCALIZATION-INJECTIVE-001`

The localization map

`S_T(Q) -> product_v H^1(Q_v,T)`

is injective.

### Proof

By Lemma `BSD-A1-WP24-COMPACT-SELMER-001`, it is enough to detect the `Z_2`-line generated by `P` at the place `2`.

The compact Kummer map at `2` identifies the `2`-adic completion of `E(Q_2)` with the compact Kummer local condition inside `H^1(Q_2,T)`. The group `E(Q_2)` contains an open formal-group subgroup isomorphic, as a topological `Z_2`-module, to an open subgroup of `Z_2`. Hence

`intersection_{n>=1} 2^n E(Q_2)`

consists only of prime-to-`2` torsion: on the open `Z_2` subgroup the intersection is zero, while multiplication by `2` is an automorphism on the odd-primary finite quotient.

Suppose the continuous map

`Z_2 t_P -> H^1(Q_2,T)`

had nonzero kernel. Every nonzero closed subgroup of `Z_2` is `2^r Z_2` for some `r>=0`, so `2^r t_P` would map to zero. Equivalently, `2^r P` would lie in `2^nE(Q_2)` for every `n`; therefore `2^rP` would be prime-to-`2` torsion in `E(Q_2)`. But `P` has infinite order in `E(Q)` and hence in `E(Q_2)`, contradiction.

Thus the place-`2` localization is injective on `S_T(Q)`, and so is the full localization map. QED.

### Corollary `BSD-A1-WP24-DUAL-IMAGE-001`

The compact dual-Selmer localization image is the free rank-one `Z_2` module

`G^* intersect U^* = Z_2 loc(t_P)`.

Define

`chi_P:Q_Sigma -> Q_2/Z_2`

`chi_P(z) := <z,loc(t_P)>_loc`.

### Theorem `BSD-A1-WP24-ANNIHILATOR-001`

`im(gamma) = ker(chi_P)`.

### Proof

## 7. The remaining scalar invariant

Define

`rho_E := len_Z2 im(chi_P|K_loc)`.

The image is a finite cyclic `2`-primary subgroup of `Q_2/Z_2`, so `rho_E` is a well-defined nonnegative integer.

### Theorem `BSD-A1-WP24-LENGTH-001`

One has

`len_Z2(C_E^vee)
 = len_Z2(K_loc^vee) - rho_E`

and therefore

`len_Z2(C_E^vee)
 = 2 ord_2(3-a_2)
   + sum_{ell|N} ord_2(c_ell)
   - rho_E`.

Equivalently,

`Fitt^0_Z2(C_E^vee)
 = 2^{2 ord_2(3-a_2)
       + sum_{ell|N} ord_2(c_ell)
       - rho_E} Z_2`.

### Proof

Pontryagin duality is exact on finite `2`-primary groups. Dualizing the short exact sequence in Theorem `BSD-A1-WP24-GLOBAL-HIT-001` gives

`0 -> im(chi_P|K_loc)^vee
   -> K_loc^vee
   -> C_E^vee
   -> 0`.

Length is additive in a short exact sequence of finite `Z_2`-modules. Thus

`len_Z2(C_E^vee)
 = len_Z2(K_loc^vee)
   - len_Z2 im(chi_P|K_loc)`.

The second term is `rho_E` by definition. Protected WP23 gives the exact formula for `len_Z2(K_loc^vee)`, yielding the stated identity.

For any finite `Z_2`-module `M`,

`Fitt^0_Z2(M)=2^{len_Z2(M)}Z_2`.

Applying this to `C_E^vee` gives the Fitting-ideal statement. QED.

## 8. Generator independence

### Lemma `BSD-A1-WP24-BASIS-INVARIANCE-001`

The subgroup `ker(chi_P|K_loc)`, the image order `#im(chi_P|K_loc)`, and the integer `rho_E` do not depend on the chosen saturated generator.

### Proof

Any other `Z_2`-basis `t_P'` of `S_T(Q)` has the form

`t_P' = u t_P`

for some `u in Z_2^x`. Bilinearity gives

`chi_P' = u chi_P`.

Multiplication by a `2`-adic unit is an automorphism of `Q_2/Z_2` and of each finite `2`-primary subgroup. Hence `chi_P'` and `chi_P` have the same kernel on `K_loc` and images of the same order. Therefore `rho_E` is basis-independent. QED.
