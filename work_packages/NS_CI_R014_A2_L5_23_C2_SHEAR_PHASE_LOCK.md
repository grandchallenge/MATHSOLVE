target transfer for one fixed positive turnover interval.  The next possible

resetting or phase decoherence and therefore incur an additional cost.

occur at all.

Take an exact decaying horizontal shear and a vertical passive component.  In
turnover variables the vertical Fourier coefficients form a viscous
nearest-neighbour chain.  The subspace

\`\`\`math
a_n(\tau)=(-i)^n b_n(\tau),
\qquad
b_n(\tau)\in\mathbb R,
\`\`\`

is exactly invariant for every positive viscosity and for the time-dependent
decay of the shear.

Therefore every adjacent pair remains in exact quadrature whenever both
coefficients are nonzero:

\`\`\`math
\operatorname{Re}(\overline{a_n}a_{n+1})=0,
\qquad
\frac{
|\operatorname{Im}(\overline{a_n}a_{n+1})|
}{
|a_n||a_{n+1}|
}
=
1.
\`\`\`

The transfer correlation is thus **maximally phase coherent**.  Viscosity can
damp the real amplitudes \`b_n\`; amplitudes can cross zero and reverse transfer
direction; but no gradual phase decoherence is generated by this exact chain.

In the inviscid turnover limit the solution is explicit:

\`\`\`math
a_n(\tau)
=
(-i)^n J_n(\tau),
\`\`\`

so the exact phase locking persists across the full Bessel cascade.

Hence a generic C2 theorem cannot obtain a repeated-event cost merely by

## 8. Phase reversals do not require phase decoherence

When \`b_n\` changes sign, the complex coefficient \`a_n\` changes by a phase
of \`\pi\`, but it does so by passing through zero **inside the same invariant
real line** \`(-i)^n\mathbb R\`.

The adjacent phase relation remains either \`+\pi/2\` or \`-\pi/2\` whenever
both coefficients are nonzero.

Hence transfer direction can reverse without a gradual loss of phase
coherence.

A proposed repeat-cost theorem of the form

\`\`\`text

progressive phase decorrelation
    =>
non-summable cost
\`\`\`

is false in this calibration.

Any cost of reversal must be associated with amplitude zero-crossing,
dissipation, regeneration of amplitude, or another dynamical quantity.
