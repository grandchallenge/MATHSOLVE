# FIGURATE WP09-030 — complete narrow Picard groups in the square-conductor subfamily

**Scope:** This work computes the full narrow Picard group of the nonmaximal quadratic order \(\mathcal O_f=\mathbb Z[f\sqrt2]\) for every positive odd \(f\), with a complete finite algorithm and strict resource guards. This is the FIGURATE anchor subfamily \(A=8m+1=f^2\), \(m=(f^2-1)/8\). Combining the result with protected WP09-029 determines the **order** of the full oriented ray group. ## Theorem A — conductor formula for the complete narrow Picard group

Fix positive odd \(f\). In \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), the maximal order \(\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\) is norm-Euclidean, and \(\varepsilon=1+\sqrt2\) has norm \(-1\). Hence the maximal-order *narrow* class group is trivial. Its group of totally positive units is generated by \(\varepsilon^2=3+2\sqrt2\).

The conductor order is \(\mathcal O_f=\mathbb Z+f\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[f\sqrt2]\). The standard narrow conductor exact sequence yields the **full** group isomorphism

$$
\operatorname{Pic}^{+}(\mathcal O_f)\cong
\frac{(\mathcal O_K/f\mathcal O_K)^\times}
{(\mathbb Z/f\mathbb Z)^\times
 \cdot\operatorname{im}\langle3+2\sqrt2\rangle}.
$$

**Proof.** Extension of invertible ideals to the maximal order induces a surjective map to its narrow Picard group, trivial here. Prime-to-conductor extension and contraction show the kernel is made of conductor residue units, modulo scalar residues and residues of globally totally positive maximal-order units. Their exact groups give the displayed quotient. Working in the integral conductor order (not silently substituting its maximal order at the ray modulus) retains the correct nonmaximal-order arithmetic. QED.

The numerator is the unit group of \((\mathbb Z/f)[T]/(T^2-2)\). It has exact multiplication

$$
(x,y)(u,v)=(xu+2yv,\;xv+yu)\pmod f.
$$

A pair is a unit precisely when \(\gcd(x^2-2y^2,f)=1\). The denominator is generated by scalar units \((s,0)\) and \((3,2)\). Thus the quotient's entire abelian group law and invariant factors are computable by finite exact arithmetic, without enumerating norm-equation representatives or seed entries.

## Theorem B — independent class-number identity

For odd primes \(p\mid f\), write \(\chi_8(p)=(2/p)\in\{-1,1\}\). Let \(t(f)\) be the least positive exponent \(n\) for which the coefficient of \(\sqrt2\) in \((1+\sqrt2)^n\) is divisible by \(f\). This is the order of the image of \(\varepsilon\) modulo scalar units. Then the image of the totally positive unit \(\varepsilon^2\) has order

$$
t^+(f)=\frac{t(f)}{\gcd(2,t(f))}.
$$

At a conductor prime power \(p^e\), the size of the residue unit group modulo scalar units is \(p^e(1-\chi_8(p)/p)\). The Chinese remainder theorem gives

$$
Q(f)=\left|\frac{(\mathcal O_K/f)^\times}{(\mathbb Z/f)^\times}\right|
=f\prod_{p\mid f}\left(1-\frac{\chi_8(p)}p\right).
$$

Consequently the *ordinary* and *narrow* Picard numbers are respectively

$$
h(\mathcal O_f)=\frac{Q(f)}{t(f)},\qquad
h^+(\mathcal O_f)=\frac{Q(f)}{t^+(f)}.
$$

These are exact independent checks on the finite quotient algorithm. In particular \(h^+/h=\gcd(2,t)\): the conductor order admits a norm-negative unit exactly when \(t\) is odd. QED.

## Theorem C — full oriented ray **order** for all square-\(A\) parameters

For this subfamily the FIGURATE ray modulus is \(A\mathcal O_f=f^2\mathcal O_f\), not \(A\mathcal O_K\). Protected WP09-029 computes the entire oriented ray-unit kernel \(K_A\), and its exact sequence is

$$
1\to K_A\to
\operatorname{Cl}^{+}_{A\mathcal O_f}(\mathcal O_f)
\to\operatorname{Pic}^{+}(\mathcal O_f)\to1.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\left|\operatorname{Cl}^{+}_{A\mathcal O_f}(\mathcal O_f)\right|
=|K_A|\,h^+(\mathcal O_f).
}
$$

The identity determines the **group order**, not necessarily its invariant factors or extension law. The algorithm explicitly refuses to report a complete full ray-group presentation for \(f>3\). This separates a complete narrow Picard calculation from a still-open extension computation.
