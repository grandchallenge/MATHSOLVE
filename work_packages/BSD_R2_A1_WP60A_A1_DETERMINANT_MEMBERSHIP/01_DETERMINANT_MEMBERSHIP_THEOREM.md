# WP60A-A1 theorem — determinant membership equals Fitting divisibility

## 1. Setup

Let `R` be a discrete valuation ring with fraction field `K`, uniformizer `pi`, and valuation `v` normalized by `v(pi)=1`.

Let

`C=[P^1 --d--> P^2]`

be a two-term perfect complex concentrated in cohomological degrees `1` and `2`, where `P^1` and `P^2` are finite free `R`-modules.

Assume:

1. `H^1(C)=ker(d)` is free of rank one;
2. `H^2(C)=coker(d)` is finite.

Then `rank_R(P^1)=rank_R(P^2)+1`.

Write

`Delta_R(C):=det_R^{-1}(C)
 = det_R(P^1) tensor det_R(P^2)^{-1}`.

After tensoring with `K`, `H^2(C)_K=0`, so the standard determinant trivialization gives a canonical `K`-linear isomorphism, up to the harmless determinant sign convention,

`lambda_C : Delta_R(C) tensor_R K -> H^1(C) tensor_R K`.

Only the resulting rank-one `R`-lattice is used below, so the sign has no effect.

## 2. The determinant-lattice theorem

### Theorem `BSD-A1-WP60A-A1-DET-LATTICE-001`

Under the setup above,

`lambda_C(Delta_R(C))
 = Fitt^0_R(H^2(C)) * H^1(C)`

as rank-one `R`-lattices inside `H^1(C) tensor_R K`.

### Proof

Set `m=rank_R(P^2)`, so `rank_R(P^1)=m+1`.

By Smith normal form there are bases

`e_1,...,e_m,e_{m+1}` of `P^1`

and

`f_1,...,f_m` of `P^2`

such that

`d(e_i)=d_i f_i` for `1<=i<=m`,

`d(e_{m+1})=0`,

with each nonzero `d_i in R` and `d_i | d_{i+1}`.

Then

`H^1(C)=R e_{m+1}`

and

`H^2(C)= direct_sum_{i=1}^m R/(d_i)`.

Hence

`Fitt^0_R(H^2(C))=(d_1 ... d_m)`.

A basis of the inverse determinant line is

`delta=(e_1 wedge ... wedge e_m wedge e_{m+1})
       tensor (f_1 wedge ... wedge f_m)^{-1}`.

Over `K`, the summand generated by `e_1,...,e_m` maps isomorphically to `P^2_K`. The canonical acyclic determinant trivialization contributes the determinant of this isomorphism, namely `d_1...d_m`, up to sign. Therefore

`lambda_C(delta)= +/- (d_1...d_m) e_{m+1}`.

Thus the image lattice is exactly

`(d_1...d_m) R e_{m+1}
 = Fitt^0_R(H^2(C)) H^1(C)`.

QED.

## 3. Membership and generator corollaries

Let `P` be a primitive basis of `H^1(C)` and let

`z=aP`

be a nonzero class in `H^1(C)`.

Write

`n=length_R H^2(C)`.

Since `R` is a DVR,

`Fitt^0_R(H^2(C))=(pi^n)`.

### Corollary `BSD-A1-WP60A-A1-MEMBERSHIP-001`

The following are equivalent:

1. `z` belongs to `lambda_C(Delta_R(C))`;
2. `a in Fitt^0_R(H^2(C))`;
3. `(a) subseteq Fitt^0_R(H^2(C))`;
4. `v(a)>=n`;
5. `length_R(H^1(C)/Rz)>=length_R H^2(C)`.

Hence determinant membership is exactly a one-sided Fitting divisibility.

### Corollary `BSD-A1-WP60A-A1-PRIMITIVITY-001`

The following are equivalent:

1. `z=lambda_C(delta)` for some basis `delta` of the rank-one module `Delta_R(C)`;
2. `(a)=Fitt^0_R(H^2(C))`;
3. `v(a)=n`;
4. `length_R(H^1(C)/Rz)=length_R H^2(C)`.

Hence determinant primitivity is equality, not merely membership.

## 4. Height-one localization

Let `Lambda=Z_2[[T]]` and let

`q=(2)`.

Then `Lambda_q` is a DVR with uniformizer `2`; `T` is a unit in `Lambda_q`.

Suppose a perfect Iwasawa cohomology complex `C_Iw` has, after localization at `q`,

- `H^1(C_Iw)_q` torsion-free of rank one;
- `H^2(C_Iw)_q` torsion of finite length.

Then `H^1(C_Iw)_q` is free rank one over the DVR `Lambda_q`, and the theorem applies verbatim:

`lambda_q(det^{-1}(C_Iw)_q)
 = Fitt^0_{Lambda_q}(H^2(C_Iw)_q) H^1(C_Iw)_q`.

For a nonzero localized analytic class `z_q=a_q P_q`, determinant lift is equivalent to

`ord_q(a_q)>=length_{Lambda_q} H^2(C_Iw)_q`,

and determinant-generator status is equality.
