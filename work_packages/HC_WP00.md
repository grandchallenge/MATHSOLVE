## Lay companion

A projective variety has two ways to produce middle-dimensional information.

One way is geometric: choose subvarieties, combine them with rational coefficients, and record how they sit inside the ambient variety. The other way is analytic-topological: decompose cohomology into Hodge types using complex differential forms.

Every algebraic subvariety lands in the diagonal type `(p,p)`. The Hodge conjecture asks whether every rational cohomology class on that diagonal came from algebraic geometry.

The word rational matters. Integral classes can contain torsion obstructions and the integral statement is false. The word projective matters. Compact Kahler manifolds still have Hodge decomposition, but the unrestricted analogous algebraicity statement is false. The problem is therefore not merely to recognize the right bidegree; it is to construct enough algebraic cycles under the exact projective and rational hypotheses.

## Formal target

Let `X` be a smooth projective algebraic variety over `C`, of complex dimension `n`. Hodge decomposition gives

```math
H^k(X,C)=\bigoplus_{a+b=k} H^{a,b}(X),
\qquad \overline{H^{a,b}}=H^{b,a}.
```

For `0<=p<=n`, define

```math
Hdg^{2p}(X,Q)=H^{2p}(X,Q)\cap H^{p,p}(X).
```

Let `Z^p(X)` be the free abelian group on irreducible codimension-`p` subvarieties. The cycle class map

```math
cl^p: Z^p(X)->H^{2p}(X,Z)
```

kills rational equivalence and therefore factors through `CH^p(X)`. With rational coefficients:

```math
cl_Q^p: CH^p(X)\otimes_Z Q -> Hdg^{2p}(X,Q).
```

The classical Hodge conjecture is the assertion that `cl_Q^p` is surjective for every `X` and `p`.

## Exact equivalence

The following statements are equivalent for fixed `X,p`:

1. `cl_Q^p` is surjective.
2. Every class in `Hdg^{2p}(X,Q)` equals a rational linear combination of classes of codimension-`p` irreducible algebraic subvarieties.

Reason: `Z^p(X)` is generated by the irreducible codimension-`p` subvarieties, and the cohomological class factors through the quotient `CH^p(X)`. Passing from `Z^p(X) tensor Q` to `CH^p(X) tensor Q` changes representatives but not the image in cohomology.

This equivalence does not assert effectivity, uniqueness of a cycle, or equality modulo rational equivalence for two cycles with the same cohomology class.

## Elementary boundary cases

### `p=0`

Codimension-zero cycles are combinations of irreducible components. For connected smooth projective `X`, `Hdg^0(X,Q)=Q` is generated by `[X]`.

### `p=n`

Top-degree rational cohomology is generated componentwise by point classes.

### `p=1`

The Lefschetz `(1,1)` theorem identifies integral degree-two `(1,1)` classes with first Chern classes of holomorphic line bundles, hence with divisor classes. Tensoring with `Q` gives the rational Hodge statement in codimension one.

### `p=n-1`

Let `h` be a hyperplane class. Hard Lefschetz gives an isomorphism of rational Hodge structures

```math
L^{n-2}:H^2(X,Q)->H^{2n-2}(X,Q),
\qquad \alpha\mapsto h^{n-2}\cup\alpha.
```

A rational Hodge class in degree `2n-2` has a rational `(1,1)` preimage. By Lefschetz `(1,1)`, that preimage is a rational divisor class; multiplying the divisor by `h^{n-2}` gives an algebraic codimension-`n-1` cycle class.

### Dimension consequence

For `n<=3`, every codimension is one of `0,1,n-1,n`. Thus the classical Hodge conjecture holds for all smooth projective varieties of complex dimension at most three. The first unrestricted case not covered by this argument is `n=4,p=2`.

## Statement lattice

The canonical node is rational cycle-class surjectivity. The following are not equivalent substitutions:

- Integral Hodge: stronger and false in general.
- Compact-Kahler analogue: broader geometric domain and false in general.
- Generalized Hodge: coniveau/support statement, distinct and stronger in the relevant direction.
- Variational Hodge: deformation statement in families.
- Hodge-locus algebraicity: a theorem about parameter spaces, not cycle construction.
- Absolute Hodge: stability under field embeddings; algebraic classes are absolute, but the converse is not the classical theorem.
- Motivated: broadened correspondence class used to build an unconditional motivic category.
- Lefschetz standard conjecture: algebraicity of inverse Lefschetz correspondences.
- Tate: arithmetic Galois-invariant analogue requiring comparison and lifting bridges.
- Effective-cycle statement: stronger than rational linear-combination generation.

## Proof-obligation template for any restricted target

A theorem-grade target must supply:

1. **Typed input:** an arbitrary `alpha in Hdg^{2p}(X,Q)` under a precise variety-class restriction.
2. **Construction:** a finite rational cycle `z=sum_i q_i[Z_i]` with each `Z_i` algebraic of codimension `p`.
3. **Exact comparison:** a proof that `cl_Q^p(z)=alpha` in rational cohomology.
4. **Quantifier closure:** the construction works for every allowed `X,p,alpha`, not only a very general member or numerically sampled class.
5. **Dependency disclosure:** every projector, correspondence, deformation, specialization, or lifting result is named and sourced.
6. **Coefficient control:** denominators, torsion, and integral/rational changes are explicit.
7. **Cycle-equivalence control:** cohomological equality is not confused with rational equivalence or uniqueness in the Chow group.

## Failure ledger

A route terminates at WP00 when it produces only:

- a necessary Hodge-type or period condition;
- an algebraic Hodge locus without a relative cycle;
- a topological or motivated correspondence not proved algebraic;
- a Tate class without a characteristic-zero lifting theorem;
- a numerical lattice relation without exact certification;
- a cycle on a generic fiber without control of special fibers;
- a theorem conditional on the very standard conjecture or variational statement that supplies the missing algebraicity.

Such results may remain useful intermediate artifacts, but their conclusion must be named accurately.
