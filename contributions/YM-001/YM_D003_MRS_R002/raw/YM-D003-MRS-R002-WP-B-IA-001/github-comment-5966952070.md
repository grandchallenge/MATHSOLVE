## Strongest exact statement

Fix one infrared regulator R of the type used by Magnen–Rivasseau–Sénéor, fix one Slavnov-hierarchy order N, and fix smooth test functions for all external variables. Let S_k^(rho,R) denote the normalized cutoff Schwinger distributions that occur in the N-th identity, and let L_(N,R) be the finite distributional differential/contraction operator forming the left-hand side of MRS Eq. (VIII.6). Suppose, only for the finitely many k and test functions generated by this fixed identity,

(1) S_k^(rho,R) -> S_k^R distributionally (equivalently, it is enough that every smeared scalar pairing actually occurring in this one identity converges);

(2) at finite ultraviolet cutoff rho the exact regulated identity is
    L_(N,R)(S^(rho,R)) = E_(N,R) + delta_(N,R)(rho)
after smearing, where E_(N,R) is the fixed-infrared-cutoff correction in Eq. (VIII.6); and

(3) delta_(N,R)(rho) -> 0 distributionally (equivalently, for this fixed test tuple, its scalar pairing tends to zero).

Then
    L_(N,R)(S^R) = E_(N,R)
for that fixed N and fixed test tuple.

No bound uniform in N and no control uniform in the infrared regulator is required for this finite-order passage. MRS state precisely this mechanism at the end of Sect. VIII: the cutoff identity has right-hand side E_N + delta_N(rho); the cutoff Schwinger functions tend to the constructed no-UV-cutoff Schwinger functions; and delta_N(rho) tends to zero. See Magnen–Rivasseau–Sénéor, Commun. Math. Phys. 155 (1993), Sect. VIII, especially Eq. (VIII.6) and pp. 377–378. The introduction, p. 328, states the resulting UV limit and Slavnov identities while explicitly noting that a detailed proof of the full construction is not supplied.

## Derivation

Work at fixed R, N, and fixed smooth test tuple Phi. If the infrared regularization is a compact manifold/torus, take Phi in the corresponding C^infinity test space; if it is a finite box, take the test functions smooth with support/allowed boundary behavior appropriate to that fixed box. Equivalently one may state the argument in the standard distribution space D'(X_R^k), where X_R denotes the fixed regulated spacetime.

MRS explicitly say that the identities are to be understood after application to test functions (immediately after their two-point example (VIII.4)), so no pointwise product or pointwise field value is needed.

For this fixed hierarchy member, the left side of (VIII.6) contains only finitely many normalized Schwinger-function terms, with fixed index contractions and spacetime derivatives. Distributional differentiation is continuous: if T_rho -> T in D', then for every multi-index alpha and test function phi,
    <partial^alpha T_rho, phi>
      = (-1)^|alpha| <T_rho, partial^alpha phi>
      -> (-1)^|alpha| <T, partial^alpha phi>
      = <partial^alpha T, phi>.
Finite index contractions, permutations of variables, and finite sums are also continuous linear operations. Therefore assumption (1) gives
    <L_(N,R)(S^(rho,R)), Phi>
      -> <L_(N,R)(S^R), Phi>.

At finite rho, assumption (2) gives the scalar equality
    <L_(N,R)(S^(rho,R)), Phi>
      = <E_(N,R), Phi> + <delta_(N,R)(rho), Phi>.

The infrared regulator R is held fixed throughout, so E_(N,R) is fixed in the rho-limit; there is no interchange of an infrared and ultraviolet limit. By assumption (3),
    <delta_(N,R)(rho), Phi> -> 0.
Taking the ordinary limit of the preceding scalar equality therefore yields
    <L_(N,R)(S^R), Phi> = <E_(N,R), Phi>.

Because Phi was arbitrary in the chosen test space, if (1) and (3) hold in D' rather than merely for one fixed tuple, the distributional identity L_(N,R)(S^R)=E_(N,R) follows.

This argument uses no compactness, no subsequence extraction, no exchange of infinite sums with limits, and no uniform-in-N estimate. In particular, the finite-order Slavnov passage itself carries no additional constructive-field-theory proof debt beyond establishing the two convergence premises. MRS's detailed proof debt lies upstream: their paper says that the expansion proves delta_N(rho)->0 and that the cutoff Schwinger functions tend to the constructed functions, but also says that convergence proofs of the overall expansion are not supplied in all detail.

## Assumptions beyond bootstrap

For theorem-grade typing of the implication, the source's statements "the normalized Schwinger functions with cutoff rho ... tend" and "delta_N(rho) tends to zero" are taken in the following minimal sense: for fixed R and fixed N, every smeared distributional pairing occurring in (VIII.6) converges, and the corresponding smeared defect tends to zero. Full D'-convergence is sufficient but stronger than necessary.

No assumption of infrared-cutoff removal, uniformity in R, uniformity over N, OS positivity, hierarchy summability, or mass gap is made.

## Claim boundary

This proves only the logical passage from one fixed-order, fixed-IR regulated Slavnov identity to its ultraviolet limit once the required smeared Schwinger convergence and vanishing defect are established. It does not prove those constructive convergence estimates themselves; MRS explicitly state that detailed convergence proofs are not all supplied. It gives no simultaneous hierarchy-level convergence, no uniform infrared control or IR removal, no complete Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction, no nontrivial-topology result, no mass gap, and no YM-001 closure.
